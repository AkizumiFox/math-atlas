    Notation

    This page is a reference, not a section: there is nothing to work through here, and it carries no exercises and no quick checks. Use it when you meet a symbol and want to know what it means and where the book fixed it. The symbols are grouped by the kind of thing they name, not by chapter, because a reader arrives with a symbol and not with a chapter number. Every entry links to the place where the book defines it; follow the link for the hypotheses, which a one-line gloss cannot carry.

    Read the conventions first. Most of what looks like a clash between two symbols is resolved by one of them.

    The standing conventions

    The field. F is an arbitrary field throughout, and Fp is the field with p elements (Definition 0.7.1). Chapters 11 to 13 and much of Part V need R or C, and say so where they do. Vector spaces are V,U,W, plain italic, and are finite-dimensional unless the statement says otherwise.

    Bold is for matrices and coordinate vectors, never for maps, spaces, or abstract vectors. A matrix is a bold upright capital, A; a coordinate vector, an element of Fn, is a bold lowercase letter, v; an element of an abstract vector space is plain italic, v, so the letter shows whether a computation is in coordinates or in the structure. The test is exact: an object is bold only when it is an element of a fixed, finite-dimensional Fn or Fm×n viewed in coordinates. So the zero matrix is bold, O, as every matrix is; the zero vector is bold in Fn and plain, 0, in an abstract space; and a sequence in FN, a coset v+U, and the quaternion units i,j,k are plain. A coset’s representative keeps its own type, so in Fn/U one writes x+U; an operator or linear map is plain italic, T; a space is plain italic, V. Entries stay plain: aij, or (A)ij. A Greek letter naming a matrix is bold too, as Λ or Σ.

    Maps and composition. Linear maps are T,S:V→W, never f. Composition is written ST, and it means: first T, then S. For functions in general the book writes g∘f.

    Coordinates. [v]B is the coordinate vector of v in the ordered basis B (Definition 1.5.2), and [T]BC is the matrix of T with input basis B and output basis C (Definition 4.1.1). The subscript is the basis read into the first slot and the superscript the basis read into the second: for a map that is the input basis below and the output basis above, and for a bilinear form [β]BB it is the basis used on the first argument below and the one used on the second above. Both slots are always written out, even when they hold the same basis. A basis is an ordered list, written in parentheses.

    Transpose and adjoint. A⊤ is the transpose (Definition 0.9.10) and A∗ the conjugate transpose (Definition 11.1.2), which is also the symbol for the adjoint of an operator (Definition 11.6.1). The prime T′ is the dual map (Definition 5.4.1), and never a derivative of a map.

    Inner products are linear in the first slot and conjugate-linear in the second (Definition 11.1.1). Over R the distinction disappears.

    Ordering of eigenvalues and singular values. For Hermitian A, the eigenvalues are listed decreasing, λ1(A)≥⋯≥λn(A), with multiplicity. Singular values are listed decreasing as well, σ1(A)≥σ2(A)≥⋯≥0 (Definition 13.9.1). Where a proof wants the increasing order it says so.

    Indices start at 1, with three announced exceptions: Chapter 12 §09, where circulant and Fourier subscripts run from 0 to n−1 and are read modulo n; Chapter 22 §§06 to 10, where the coordinates of Fn+1 run from 0 so that Pn(F) has dimension n (Definition 22.6.2); and Chapter 23 §09, which follows Chapter 12 §09 for group elements and characters.

    Defining, not asserting. := means “is defined to be”. A plain = asserts an equality that has been or will be proved.

    Where the book overrides itself

    Twenty-four chapters do not have twenty-four alphabets. Several letters carry more than one meaning, and a few English words carry more than one definition. In every case the local meaning is declared where it is used, and in every case there is a tell — an argument, a subscript, a typeface — that decides which one is meant. The rows below list the clashes worth knowing before you meet them.
