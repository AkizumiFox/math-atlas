Math Atlas

A series of textbooks for a reader working alone, from a first proof to the graduate qualifying exam in mathematics.

Each volume is a book of its own, with its own contents, search and exercises. A reference to another volume links straight to it, and hovering over the link shows the cited result without leaving the page.

Work in progress: these are drafts, rebuilt from the current branches, and chapters appear as they are written.

Reading order

Where to start, and what each volume needs.

Foundations

Proof, sets and numbers: what every later volume takes for granted, taught from the beginning.

  • F1

    Logic and Proof

    Logic as the grammar of proof, for a reader who has never been asked to write one: derivations, rule by rule, for the connectives and quantifiers; truth tables, soundness and completeness; and the way back from derivations to ordinary mathematical prose.

    5 of 5 chapters written · 39 sections

    Read

  • F2

    Sets, Relations, and Cardinality

    Sets and functions, relations and orders, induction and recursion, and the sizes of infinite sets, from diagonal arguments to the Schröder–Bernstein theorem. Chapter 3 ends with Gate 0, the examination to pass before analysis.

    3 of 4 chapters written · 19 sections

    Read

  • F3

    Number Systems: From N to R

    The number systems built out of sets: N from the ZF axioms, Z and Q as classes of pairs, divisibility and modular arithmetic, and finally R, constructed from Cauchy sequences of rationals and proved to be a complete ordered field.

    5 of 7 chapters written · 30 sections

    Read

Analysis

Calculus made rigorous, then the analysis built on it: metric spaces, several variables, measure, Fourier and complex analysis.

  • AN1

    Analysis on the Real Line

    Analysis on the real line, from the axioms of a complete ordered field: sequences and series, limits and continuity, the derivative, the Riemann–Darboux integral and power series, each proved from the least upper bound axiom.

    1 of 11 chapters written · 7 sections

    Read

Linear Algebra

A companion series, written first and published on its own site; the volumes here cite it for facts about fields and matrices.

  • LA

    Linear Algebra

    Linear algebra for a reader working alone: vector spaces and linear maps, determinants and canonical forms, inner products and spectral theory, forms and tensors, and on to representations and numerical computation.
