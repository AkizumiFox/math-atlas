Foundations

Proof, sets and numbers: what every later volume takes for granted, taught from the beginning.

  • F1Foundations

    Logic and Proof

    Logic as the grammar of proof, for a reader who has never been asked to write one: derivations, rule by rule, for the connectives and quantifiers; truth tables, soundness and completeness; and the way back from derivations to ordinary mathematical prose.

    8/8 chapters · 50 sections
    Not started in this browser

  • F2Foundations

    Sets and Functions

    Sets and functions, with proofs straight from their definitions: injections, surjections, gluing and inverses; induction and recursion; cardinality, from finite sets and the pigeonhole principle through diagonal arguments and Schröder–Bernstein to cardinal arithmetic; relations, quotients and orders; and the axiom of choice with Zorn's lemma and its equivalent forms.

    6/6 chapters · 24 sections
    Not started in this browser

    Read

  • F3Foundations

    Number Systems: From N to R

    The number systems built one from the other: N as a Peano system, with induction, recursion and order, Z and Q as classes of pairs, and finally R, constructed from Cauchy sequences of rationals and proved to be the one complete ordered field.

    6/6 chapters · 27 sections
    Not started in this browser

    Read

  • F4Foundations

    Preliminaries for Analysis and Algebra

    What analysis and algebra both use from their first pages: finite sums, inequalities and real powers, divisibility and congruences in Z, and the complex numbers built on R; then fields including Fp, groups and permutations, formal polynomials and polynomials over a field up to the fundamental theorem of algebra; and real functions and their graphs, coordinates and vectors in Rn, and lines, hyperplanes and convex sets.

    10/10 chapters · 52 sections

Analysis

Calculus made rigorous, then the analysis built on it: metric spaces, several variables, measure, Fourier and complex analysis.

  • AN1Analysis

    Analysis on the Real Line

    Analysis on the real line, from the axioms of a complete ordered field: sequences and series, limits and continuity, the derivative, the Riemann–Darboux integral and power series, each proved from the least upper bound axiom.
